Scaling the Behavior LayerSlide 11

Define a behavior tree: a root, control nodes and leaves

Goal: name the parts of a behavior tree before we read one.

Behavior tree (BT) “A directed rooted tree where the internal nodes are called control flow nodes and leaf nodes are called execution nodes” (Colledanchise and Ögren, 2018).

In plain words, with our road behaviors:

  • The root is the one node with no parent. Every tick starts there.
  • Control flow nodes, such as Sequence and Fallback, sit inside the tree and decide which child is ticked next.
  • A Condition leaf checks a fact and returns Success or Failure: a stop line within 30 m, a car ahead within 40 m.
  • An Action leaf runs a behavior: “While the action is ongoing it returns Running”. Ours: Lane keep, Follow, Change lane, Stop at the line.

Read a behavior tree: ticks, three statuses, six node types

Goal: learn how a tree runs. A tick goes down from the root: “A node is executed if and only if it receives ticks”. A ticked node returns Running, Success or Failure (Colledanchise and Ögren, 2018).

NodeWhat it does when ticked
Sequence (→)Ticks its children from the left while they succeed; stops at the first that fails or is running. Succeeds only if every child succeeds. Our pass branch: car ahead slow? left lane free? then Change lane.
Fallback (?)Ticks its children from the left while they fail; stops at the first that succeeds or is running. Fails only if every child fails. Our root: stop, pass, follow, keep lane, in that order.
ParallelTicks all its children at every tick. Succeeds once \(M\) of its \(N\) children succeed, fails once \(N - M + 1\) fail. The designer sets \(M\).
DecoratorOne child. A rule set by the designer changes the status the child returns, and when the child is ticked.

Goal: see what a path planner fixes before it samples a single candidate.

A path planner works in meters
  • Horizon, a distance: each path reaches 70 m ahead of the AV, chosen as 7 s at 10 m/s.
  • Samples, maneuver lengths: the lane change takes 20, 30 or 40 m, chosen as 2, 3 and 4 s at 10 m/s, from a sharp move to a gentle one.

More detail in the docs: why a polynomial; 10, −15, 6

  • \(u\) is the share of the distance \(L\) covered.
  • \(q(u)\) is a formula: give it \(u\), and it returns the share of the sideways move \(\Delta d\) done.
  • \(q(u) = 10u^{3} - 15u^{4} + 6u^{5}\)

\(u\)try it

Plan across the road in time: what the offset \(d(t)\) must do

  • Goal: write each candidate's sideways move as a function of time, \(d(t)\), so that every candidate is a trajectory, not a path.
  • What \(d(t)\) must do: six conditions.

Start from the AV's motion now, at \(t = 0\)
  1. \(d\)(0) = the AV's offset now
  2. \(\dot{d}\)(0) = its sideways speed now
  3. \(\ddot{d}\)(0) = its sideways acceleration now
End parallel to the target lane, at \(t = T\)
  1. \(d(T) = d_{1}\), the target offset
  2. \(\dot{d}(T) = 0\)
  3. \(\ddot{d}(T) = 0\)

  • Starting from the AV's own motion leaves no jump between two plans, 0.5 s apart.
  • And in between: be as smooth as possible, with the least jerk, how fast the acceleration changes (m/s3), which the passengers feel.
  • Many curves meet conditions 1 to 6; being smooth picks one.

More detail in the docs: the two costs, term by term

JerkThe rate of change of acceleration, in m/s3. Passengers feel a jump in acceleration as a jolt; a small jerk feels smooth.

  • \(C_{d}\), across: how much the AV jerks sideways, plus how long the move takes, plus how far it ends from the center of the lane the behavior layer picked.

    Chase a point ahead: the idea of Pure Pursuit

    • Pure Pursuit comes from pursuit guidance: steer so that you reach a point that runs ahead of you.
    • Coulter's implementation (Coulter, 1992), for a vehicle:

    1. Pick a lookahead point on the path, a distance \(L_d\) ahead.
    2. Find the circle arc from the rear axle, along the heading, through that point.
    3. Steer to follow that arc.
    • The lookahead sets the trade-off: short, and the AV tracks tightly but can weave; long, and it steers smoothly but cuts inside curves.
    • Snider: Pure Pursuit is “quite robust to large errors and discontinuous paths.
    • However, it is not clear how to pick the best look-ahead distance” (Snider, 2009).

    Summary

    Today: from a path to steering, throttle and brake
    • Behavior layer: a superstate or a behavior tree keeps it small.
    • Trajectories: paths with times, sampled in time and checked against where a moving car will be; the AV waited for car B, then passed. A quintic changes speed with small jerk.
    • Vehicle models: Ackermann for the wheels, the kinematic bicycle while the sideways acceleration stays under \(0.5\,\mu g\), the dynamic one beyond.
    • Lateral: Pure Pursuit chases a point 5 m ahead (short weaves, long cuts inside curves).
    • Longitudinal: a PID with split pedals and anti-windup; production adds a layer above it.
    • CARLA: one node, five inputs, one command, 20 times a second.
